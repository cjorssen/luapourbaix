# Scientific audit

Audit date: 2026-10-08. Catalogue edition: `2026-10-08.1`.
Scope: the 87 catalogue records, source transformations, thermodynamic closure,
limiting boundary equations, water overlay, and the three introductory tutorials.

The reaction algebra and the checked source conversions support the documented
teaching model. The catalogue is **not a homogeneous, critically evaluated
thermodynamic database**. Agreement with a compilation establishes faithful
transcription, not experimental accuracy. The outstanding qualifications below
remain part of the result, rather than being hidden behind a passing test.

## Confirmed defect and correction

The aqueous chlorine datum was stored both as a source potential and as a log K
computed using the physical Nernst factor. The fallback loader prioritizes log K.
Consequently, requesting `nernst factor=0.06` inadvertently changed its supplied
standard potential from 1.396 V to approximately 1.415837 V.

The record now retains **1.396 V as the independent source datum**. The checker
converts it using the diagram's effective factor, just as for the other source
potentials. The physical-factor default is unchanged. The catalogue edition was
advanced to identify this change.

This correction must not be applied to the manganese and hypochlorite entries
whose sources actually supply log K. For those entries the source log K remains
fixed and its equivalent potential is `s log K / n`. The new regression checks
both meanings at the physical factor and at 0.06 V. A rounded factor is a
pedagogical approximation, not a new physical temperature.

## Catalogue cycles and the copper qualification

Independent elimination of all 87 reaction vectors, together with the water
reaction, finds one dependent catalogue row. Solid phases have distinct columns:
gibbsite and amorphous aluminium hydroxide must not be equated in this test.

For the copper cycle, the stored values give

```
E0(Cu2+/Cu+) = 0.159 V
E0(Cu+/Cu)  = 0.520 V
(0.159 + 0.520)/2 = 0.3395 V
E0(Cu2+/Cu) from the separate compilation row = 0.337 V
```

The difference is 2.5 mV, or a cycle residual of approximately -0.08451749 in
log K for the direct two-electron reaction minus the two one-electron reactions
at 298.15 K. It is not numerical roundoff. The fallback policy accepts an
independent reaction basis and skips rows already determined by it; it does not
assert that all literature rows agree. Endpoint priority means that the direct
Cu2+/Cu datum can be used for a two-species diagram, whereas including Cu+
can select the two one-electron inputs and derive 0.3395 V. Thus expanding the
species set can also change the selected dataset slightly.

No number has been silently replaced by a mean or by an arbitrarily preferred
secondary table. For comparisons that require identical thermodynamic inputs,
supply one consistent independent dataset explicitly. Supplied inconsistent
redundant cycles remain errors. The cycle test records the present exception and
fails if an additional unreviewed dependency appears. A dataset with no cycles
has no redundant internal test of its numerical accuracy: independence does not
validate its measurements.

## Source conversions and physical conventions

The independent source reconstruction checks the following identities, including
all participant coefficients:

- Stepwise phosphate and carbonate dissociation constants are differences of
  cumulative protonation constants, with the appropriate direction.
- For `m M + n H2O = complex + n H+`,
  `log beta = log K(hydrolysis) - n log Kw`. The stored beta is cumulative.
- For `solid + n H+ = ion + n H2O`,
  `log Ks = log K(acid dissolution) + n log Kw`.
- Hypochlorite reduction combines minus one half of LLNL's water oxidation to
  **dissolved** oxygen with minus its hypochlorite formation reaction. Oxygen
  cancels; no gas standard state enters that conversion.
- Reversing a reaction changes the sign of log K. Redox reactions combine
  electron-weighted potentials through log K/free energy, not arithmetic means.

The source water reference is log Kw = -14. Catalogue conversions retain this
reference. Changing a diagram's Kw does not retroactively change a stored
source constant. Neither source temperature expressions nor activity-coefficient
models are evaluated.

Molality and molarity standard states have not been homogenized. No correction
based on solution density or activity coefficients has been invented. Numerical
sufficiency is not a check of common experimental standard states. A value with
many displayed digits does not acquire more accuracy through calculation.

Named phases remain gibbsite, amorphous Fe(OH)3 and Cr(OH)3, epsilon Zn(OH)2,
pyrochroite and pyrolusite, and cuprite. Entries whose sources do not identify a
polymorph remain `unspecified`; no assignment to a crystalline or amorphous form
is justified by a bare formula. Fe(OH)2 solubility is inferred from rounded
potentials, making its precipitation pH source-sensitive.

## Equations, domain construction and tutorials

For a reduction consuming n electrons and m protons, the implementation has
slope `-m s/n`; concentrations enter the reaction quotient with their balanced
powers. The aqueous–aqueous limiting balance respects atom multiplicities, so
equal-species and equal-element conventions differ for iodine and dichromate.
At aqueous–solid boundaries, the aqueous species carries the limiting element
balance; the incipient solid retains unit activity. Solid–solid equations use
unit activities without assigning finite amounts to either solid. A reaction
independent of pH is an identity or an empty locus, not a fabricated vertical
line.

The water lines obey

```
E(H2) = -s pH - (s/2) log10(pH2 / 1 bar)
E(O2) = 1.229 - s pH + (s/4) log10(pO2 / 1 bar)
```

The two default 1 bar pressures describe separate equilibria. The existence of
a thermodynamic oxidation route does not determine a corrosion rate or prove
passivation. The manual correctly distinguishes these questions.

The finalization algorithm is a reconstruction of the pairwise limiting
oxidation-state chain. It is not Gibbs-energy minimization of a fully specified
solution. Intersection events supplement the pH sweep; pair-specific replacement
equations are reused. Different limiting concentration assignments can create
ambiguities; passing this audit does not expand the model to arbitrary ligand
speciation, kinetics, mixed-valence site chemistry or nonideal solutions.

Sophie uses **aqueous** I2, not solid I2. The teaching source has an unbalanced
water coefficient in its alkaline iodate equation. The manual already uses the
balanced equation with three waters and explicitly treats the potentials as
example inputs. Adding the water reaction gives
`E0(acid) = 0.269 + 14 s = 1.0972308956 V`. The source's numerical value is not
promoted to evaluated reference data merely by correcting the equation.

Aya's six supplied constants remain unchanged. Her uranyl conversion is
`log K(acid) = -24 - 2(-14) = 4`, and the manual and explicit-reaction versions
agree on all 21 provisional boundaries. Her concentration of 1 mol/L is an
illustrative use of the ideal model, not a quantitatively validated prediction
for a concentrated uranium solution. Mineral phases and activity coefficients
would require additional physical data.

## Remaining reference-quality work

The source/conversion audit is complete at the coverage stated above. It does
not establish primary measurement provenance or uncertainty for the 24 compiled
potentials, the chlorine compilation, or all upstream database values. A future
reference-grade edition would need an explicitly selected evaluated compilation,
compatible standard states, identified solid phases, uncertainty information,
and a documented migration of affected diagrams. Retaining the current teaching
edition with these qualifications is preferable to silently combining more
unrelated tables.

## Reproducing the checks

`make check` runs the analytic, catalogue, cycle and three-format regression
suites and compiles all inline manual examples. The source-file audit is optional
and offline; obtain the three pinned upstream files separately, then run:

```sh
shasum -a 256 build/scientific-audit/*.dat
texlua tests/lua/source-audit.lua \
  tex/generic/luapourbaix/luapourbaix-database.lua \
  build/scientific-audit/phreeqc.dat \
  build/scientific-audit/minteq.v4.dat \
  build/scientific-audit/llnl.dat
```

Compare the three hashes with `database.sources()` before interpreting the
source audit. Omitting the last two files checks only the original 50 entries.
Downloaded upstream documents remain in the ignored build tree and are not
redistributed with the package.
